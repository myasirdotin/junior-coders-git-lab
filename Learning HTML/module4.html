✨ Semantic Formatting Tags (From Chapter 6)

In modern web standards, formatting tags do more than just change the appearance—they announce emotional tone and importance to search engines and screen readers:

  • <strong>: Indicates critical importance or urgency. Rendered bold by default.
  • <em>: Adds verbal stress or emphasis to a word. Rendered italic by default.
  • <mark>: Highlights text with a bright yellow background marker pen.
  • <del>: Strikes through text to show removed content (great for sale prices!).
  • <ins>: Underlines text to show newly inserted content.

🔬 Science, Math & Quotes

You can write scientific formulas and mathematical powers natively in HTML:

  • <sub>: Subscript (drops below the baseline): H<sub>2</sub>O $\rightarrow$ H2O
  • <sup>: Superscript (floats above the baseline): E = mc<sup>2</sup> $\rightarrow$ E = mc2
  • <blockquote>: Large indented block quotation for famous quotes.
  • <code>: Monospaced font for displaying programming code snippets.

🏆 Official Book Practice Exercises

🟢 Level 1: Recall (Easy)

What is the difference between <sub> and <sup>? What color does <mark> highlight by default?

Show Answer
<sub> is subscript (drops down, e.g. CO2). <sup> is superscript (floats up, e.g. 10th). <mark> highlights with yellow.

🟡 Level 2: Application (Medium)

Why do modern developers prefer <strong> and <em> instead of the older <b> and <i>?

Show Answer
<strong> and <em> are semantic tags. They tell screen readers to emphasize speech acoustically, and help search engines understand key concepts. <b> and <i> only change visuals with no meaning.
