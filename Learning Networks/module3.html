IP Addressing, CIDR & NAT 🗺️

Every device on the Internet needs an address. Master IPv4 octets, CIDR prefixes, and private home networks.

The Anatomy of an IPv4 Address

An IPv4 address consists of 32 binary bits broken into four 8-bit octets (ranging from 0 to 255):

Decimal:      192      .    168      .      1      .    100
Binary:   11000000 . 10101000 . 00000001 . 01100100

Private Networks & NAT: Saving the IPv4 Web

Because IPv4 ran out of its 4.3 billion public addresses, RFC 1918 reserved special Private IP Ranges that can never be routed directly on the public Internet:

10.0.0.0/8

Large enterprise campus networks (up to 16 million devices).

172.16.0.0/12

Mid-sized corporate and university networks.

192.168.0.0/16

Standard home Wi-Fi routers and small classroom LANs.

Network Address Translation (NAT) in your home router maps your 15 internal devices to one single public IP assigned by your ISP!

CIDR Subnet Math: The Slash Notation

The prefix /24 means the first 24 bits belong to the Network, leaving $32 - 24 = 8$ bits for Hosts:

Total Addresses = 2^HostBits = 2^8 = 256
Usable Devices = 256 - 2 = 254 usable hosts
(Address 0 is the Network ID, Address 255 is the Broadcast Address)

🧪 Your Turn: Test Your Local Gateway

Find out what private IP your local device was assigned by running:

# Windows:
ipconfig | findstr /i "IPv4"

# Linux / Mac:
ip route get 1.1.1.1

You will likely see an address like 192.168.1.x or 10.0.0.x!
