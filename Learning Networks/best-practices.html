Networking Best Practices & Security 🛡️✨

Building communication networks with diligence, integrity, and sacred trust (Amānah & Iḥsān).

1. Never Transmit Passwords or Secrets in Plaintext HTTP

Plaintext HTTP passes through coffee shop routers, public proxies, and ISPs in readable form. Always enforce HTTPS with HSTS headers.

✅ Good: https://api.oasis-aid.org (Encrypted with TLS 1.3)
❌ Bad: http://api.oasis-aid.org (Exposes passwords and tokens)

2. Restrict CORS to Explicit, Trusted Origins

Never use wildcard origins (*) for APIs that handle authentication tokens or user credentials.

✅ Good: Access-Control-Allow-Origin: https://app.oasis-aid.org
❌ Bad: Access-Control-Allow-Origin: * (on authenticated endpoints)

3. Protect Authentication Cookies with Secure & HttpOnly

Prevent cross-site script theft and ensure cookies only travel over encrypted channels.

✅ Good: Set-Cookie: session=xyz; Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict
❌ Bad: Set-Cookie: session=xyz (Vulnerable to XSS theft)

4. Respect Bandwidth & Enable Compression (*Iḥsān*)

Many students and communities in developing regions pay per megabyte on mobile connections. Enable Gzip or Brotli compression on all text responses to reduce size by 70–80%.

✅ Good: Content-Encoding: br (or gzip)
❌ Bad: Serving raw uncompressed 5MB JSON dumps

5. Firewall Unnecessary Open Ports

Never expose database ports (e.g. 3306 for MySQL, 6379 for Redis) directly to the public Internet. Keep them strictly isolated in private VPC subnets.

✅ Good: Port 443 public, Port 3306 bound to 127.0.0.1 or private subnet
❌ Bad: Binding MySQL to 0.0.0.0:3306 with no firewall
