The Internet Highway & Packets 🌐📦

Welcome to Computer Networking! Learn how physical light, electricity, and digital packets connect humanity across oceans.

The "Postcard Network" Analogy

Computers do not send entire video files or web pages as one massive uninterrupted stream. Instead, they slice data into thousands of tiny, numbered chunks called packets.

✉️

The Postcard (Packet)

Contains source IP, destination IP, sequence number, and up to 1,500 bytes of payload data.

🚦

The Switch & Router

Switches forward packets inside your local room (LAN); routers direct packets across international highways (WAN).

The Four Physical Media of Networking

Fiber-Optic Cables 💡

Flashes of laser light traveling inside glass strands across ocean floors at 200,000 km/s.

Copper Ethernet (Cat6) ⚡

Electrical voltage pulses for short, high-speed connections inside schools, offices, and homes.

Wi-Fi & Radio Waves 📡

High-frequency radio waves (2.4 GHz & 5 GHz) carrying data through the air without physical cords.

Cellular (4G / 5G) 📱

Long-range tower antennas providing mobile connectivity across towns and transit highways.

Bandwidth vs. Latency: The Water Pipe

Bandwidth (Width of Pipe): How much water can flow per second (e.g. 100 Mbps).
Latency (Delay of Pipe): How many milliseconds it takes for the first drop to arrive (e.g. 24 ms ping).

🧪 Your Turn: Ping the Wire

Open your local terminal or prompt and run a reachability test to an open educational DNS server:

# Run ping to test round-trip latency
ping 1.1.1.1

Notice the average round-trip time in milliseconds (ms). That is the speed of light bouncing across your local fiber!
