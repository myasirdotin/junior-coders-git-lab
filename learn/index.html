Our Ethical Foundation: Technology as a Sacred Trust (Amānah)

An Amānah is something placed in your care that you will one day answer for. The skill to write programs is one of them: the apps you build will hold other people's names, messages and records, and those people will trust you with them. So every lesson, example and project on Junior Coders follows four principles.

  • Amānah: trust

    People's data belongs to them, not to you. Ask only for what you need, keep passwords scrambled (hashed), and never share private details.

  • Iḥsān: excellence

    Do your best work even where no one will look: clear names, tidy code, and pages that everyone can use, including people who use a screen reader.

  • 'Ilm Nāfi': knowledge that benefits

    Build things that help people: a library catalogue, a charity tracker, a prayer-time app, a study planner.

  • Ṣidq: truthfulness

    Make screens that tell the truth, with no fake countdowns, hidden fees or tricks. Say where borrowed code came from, and never hand in someone else's work as your own.

That is also why you will not find interest calculators, gambling games or spyware anywhere in our examples.
