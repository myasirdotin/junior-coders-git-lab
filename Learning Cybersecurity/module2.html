Why length trumps complexity, how GPU clusters crack hashes, and why MFA neutralizes credential theft.

The Mathematics of Entropy

Entropy is the mathematical measure of a password's randomness, calculated in bits:

Entropy (bits) = Length × log2(PoolSize)
- Digits only (0-9): Pool = 10
- Mixed alphanumeric + symbols: Pool = 95
Length is exponential! A 24-character passphrase creates trillions of years of cracking resistance!

The Passphrase Revolution: Long & Memorable

Stop forcing frustrating 8-character jumbles like Tr$8!k. Instead, combine four random dictionary words into a memorable passphrase:

olive-trees-bloom-peacefully

This passphrase has 28 characters, over 80 bits of entropy, and takes modern supercomputers hundreds of thousands of years to brute-force!

Password Hashing: Never Plaintext (*Amānah*)

Web applications must never store passwords in plaintext. Use slow, memory-hard adaptive hashing algorithms:

// Modern PHP: Argon2id Hashing with Automatic Unique Salt
$hash = password_hash($password, PASSWORD_ARGON2ID);

// Verify safely without timing leaks:
if (password_verify($inputPassword, $hash)) {
    // Login successful!
}

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Combine two independent factors to protect accounts even if passwords leak:

1. Something You Know
Password or Passphrase
2. Something You Have
Phone Authenticator (TOTP) / Security Key
3. Something You Are
Fingerprint or Face Biometrics
