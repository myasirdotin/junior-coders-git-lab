Level 3

Hygiene, Backups & Forensics 💻🧹

Everyday habits: updates, the 3-2-1 backup rule, photo privacy, and what to do when something goes wrong.

🧼 Think of Washing Your Hands

🧠 Real-life analogy: washing your hands takes a few seconds. You do it every day. It stops many illnesses before they start.

Islam teaches that cleanliness is part of faith. Digital hygiene is the same idea for computers: small, regular habits that keep your devices and data healthy.

This module covers four habits: keeping software updated, keeping backups, protecting the hidden data in photos, and knowing what to do when something goes wrong.

🦠 Malware and Updates

Malware means "bad software": programs made to cause harm. Common kinds:

  • Virus and worm: spread from file to file or computer to computer.
  • Trojan: pretends to be a useful app, like a free game or tool, but hides harm inside.
  • Spyware: secretly watches what you do.
  • Ransomware: locks your files and demands payment to unlock them.

Malware often gets in through bugs (mistakes) in software. Companies fix them with updates, also called patches. A zero-day is a bug that attackers find before the company knows about it. Once a fix is released, the race is on: install it before attackers use the bug on you.

💡 Turn on automatic updates for your phone, computer, browser and apps. Restart when asked. Only install apps from official app stores.

💾 The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

A backup is a spare copy of your files. If your laptop breaks, is lost, or is hit by ransomware, a backup brings your work back.

3 copies of your data
  → the original + 2 backups
2 different kinds of storage
  → e.g. laptop drive + USB drive
1 copy kept in another place
  → e.g. trusted cloud storage, or a drive at
    a relative's house

🧪 Worked Example 1: Zainab's Science Project

Zainab has spent six weeks on her clean-water science project. Here is her 3-2-1 plan:

  1. Copy 1: the original folder on her laptop.
  2. Copy 2: a USB drive. She copies the folder every Friday, then unplugs the drive and puts it away.
  3. Copy 3: her school cloud drive, which is in a different place.

One day her laptop shows a ransomware message. Her files are locked. Zainab does not pay. An IT helper cleans the laptop. She restores her project from the USB drive and loses only two days of work.

Why unplugging matters: ransomware can also lock drives that are plugged in. A backup that is disconnected stays safe.

⚠️ A backup only counts if it works. Once a month, open a file from your backup to check you can really restore it.

📸 Worked Example 2: The Secret Inside a Photo

Phone photos store hidden information called metadata ("data about data"). In photos it is often called EXIF data. It can include the date, the phone model, and the exact GPS location where the photo was taken.

garden_day.jpg
  Date:      2026-09-14 10:32
  Camera:    (phone model)
  Location:  exact map position 🚩
             (could show where you live)

Omar takes a photo of his family's vegetable garden and wants to share it on the community gardening page. The location data could show strangers where his home is.

What Omar does: he uses his phone's sharing option to remove the location before sharing. Many phones have this option, and you can also turn off location for the camera app. Many big websites remove this data on upload, but not every site does, so it is safer to remove it yourself.

🚨 Worked Example 3: When Something Goes Wrong

An incident is a security problem that has really happened, such as malware or a stolen account. Incident response is the plan for dealing with it calmly.

If it happens to you:

  1. Stay calm and tell a trusted adult or the school IT team. Hiding it makes it worse.
  2. Disconnect from the internet: turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable. This stops the problem spreading.
  3. Do not pay and do not delete things. Take a photo of the screen as a record.
  4. Change your passwords using a different, clean device. Start with your email.
  5. Restore from your backup once the device has been cleaned.

Should you switch it off? Ask your IT team first. Some clues are kept only in the computer's short-term memory (RAM). They vanish when the power goes off.

How professional teams do it: many teams follow six steps, made popular by the SANS Institute. (NIST, a US standards body, publishes a similar guide.)

  1. Preparation: backups, logs and a plan, ready before anything happens.
  2. Identification: noticing that something is wrong, and checking it.
  3. Containment: stopping it spreading, for example disconnecting a computer.
  4. Eradication: removing the malware and changing passwords and keys.
  5. Recovery: restoring clean systems from backups and watching them closely.
  6. Lessons learned: asking "how do we stop this next time?" without blaming people.

Digital forensics is the careful collecting of clues about what happened, like a detective. Forensic experts copy evidence without changing it, so the facts stay trustworthy (Ṣidq).

✅ What To Do: Your Weekly Hygiene Checklist

  • ☐ Install updates and restart when asked.
  • ☐ Back up your work using 3-2-1. Test one restore each month.
  • ☐ Remove location data before sharing photos publicly.
  • ☐ Delete apps you no longer use. Fewer apps means fewer weak spots.
  • ☐ Only install apps from official stores.
  • ☐ Know who to tell if something goes wrong: a trusted adult or school IT.

🟢 Level 1: Recall

In the 3-2-1 backup rule, what do the numbers 3, 2 and 1 stand for?

Show answer
3 copies of your data, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 copy kept in another place.

🟡 Level 2: Apply

Hamza keeps his homework in a "Homework" folder and a "Homework copy" folder, both on his laptop. His USB backup drive is always plugged in. Find two problems with his plan and fix them.

Show answer
  1. Both folders are on the same laptop. If the laptop is lost or broken, both copies are gone. Fix: keep a copy in another place, such as the school cloud drive.
  2. The USB drive is always plugged in, so ransomware could lock it too. Fix: plug it in only to back up, then unplug it.
